%N AXXXXXX Squarefree numbers N with at least two prime factors such that N' + 2N is a perfect square, where N' is the arithmetic derivative (A003415).
%C AXXXXXX Motivated by Erdos Problem #307: (sum 1/p over P)(sum 1/q over Q) = 1 for finite prime sets P, Q is equivalent to a 2-cycle of the arithmetic derivative, and the product N = M(P)M(Q) of any solution satisfies that BOTH N'-2N and N'+2N are perfect squares (split-discriminant criterion). This sequence is the plus condition alone; the minus condition has no instances below 10^112 (it forces sum of prime reciprocals of N to be >= 2).
%C AXXXXXX Semiprime terms N = p*q satisfy (2p+1)(2q+1) = 2s^2+1 and necessarily p == q == 1 (mod 4) [proof: -2 must be a QR modulo every prime factor of 2p+1 and 2q+1].
%C AXXXXXX General local law: for every odd prime l dividing N, the cofactor N/l is a nonzero quadratic residue mod l (verified for all terms).
%C AXXXXXX Conjecturally infinite: for p = 5, q = (s^2-5)/11 with s even, s == +-4 (mod 11) automatically satisfies q == 1 (mod 4); Bunyakovsky's conjecture for this quadratic gives infinitely many terms 5q.
%C AXXXXXX The counting function is approximately 0.25*sqrt(X) (square-density heuristic; ratios 0.250-0.255 observed to 2*10^5).
%F AXXXXXX For semiprime terms N = p*q with s^2 = N' + 2N: (2p+1)(2q+1) = 2s^2 + 1.
%e AXXXXXX 145 = 5*29 is a term: 145' = 34, and 34 + 290 = 324 = 18^2.
